# voice-rules.md — how Amit writes

Read this every time you invoke `tiny-trauma-content`. Memorise the cadence
before writing a word.

## The four sacred rules

1. **Italics for emphasis, never bold.** Bold doesn't exist inside an essay
   body. If a word is doing emotional work, italic it. Use `*asterisks*` in
   markdown.
2. **Lowercase is intentional.** Titles use sentence case. The wordmark and
   nav are fully lowercase. Don't auto-capitalize. The reader is supposed
   to feel like they're reading a letter, not a publication.
3. **The handwritten font is rare.** One inline `==phrase==` per essay max.
   One block `[[aside]] line` per essay max. It's the writer's margin note,
   not decoration. If you can cut both, cut both.
4. **No emoji. Ever. Even in the terminal output.**

## Tone, in one paragraph

Dry, slightly literary, self-aware, a little tired but never bitter. Amit
notices small things — the auto-rickshaw that didn't say yes or no, the
friend he owes a haircut, the noodle commercial that cracked him open on a
Tuesday. He has theories. He distrusts them. The essays argue with
themselves. They don't land where they're expected to. There is no advice.
There is no take. There is only the noticing, and the small uncomfortable
admission that comes after it.

## Sample paragraphs — read these before writing

From *Why I cry at ads but not at things that matter*:

> The first time I noticed it, I was thirty-one, alone, and the commercial
> was for a brand of instant noodles I have never bought and will never
> buy. A father walks his daughter to the bus stand on her first day at a
> hostel. She forgets her steel tiffin in the kitchen. He runs back, six
> floors, no lift, with the tiffin. She does not turn around. He stands
> there. The bus leaves. He stands there with the tiffin. I cried for
> forty seconds, then for another twenty when the jingle came back.

From *Bangalore auto and the philosophy of maybe*:

> The 8:47 auto-rickshaw didn't really say no, but it didn't say yes
> either, and somewhere inside that ambiguity I had to live for the next
> eleven minutes. Bangalore traffic is mostly weather; you don't ask the
> cloud whether it intends to rain.

Notice:
- Specific times, specific objects (steel tiffin, 8:47, six floors, no lift).
- Sentences vary in length, then suddenly stack short ones for rhythm.
- No filler — *"It was a Tuesday"* not *"This happened on a Tuesday"*.
- A small uncomfortable admission at the end of each scene.
- Italics only on the load-bearing word, never two in a row.

## Titles

Sentence case. The emotional word is italicised at the right place.

Good:
- "Why I cry at ads but not at things *that matter*"
- "Bangalore auto and the philosophy of *maybe*"
- "On leaving messages on *read*"
- "I owe a friend an apology and *a haircut*"
- "I have been performing *competence* for fourteen years"
- "The 3 a.m. *cart*"
- "A grandmother forgets the word for *kettle*"

Avoid:
- All-lowercase or all-uppercase titles
- Titles ending in `:` followed by a subtitle (clickbait shape)
- Two italic words in one title
- Titles that are takes ("Why X is killing Y")
- Titles that promise a list ("5 things…")
- "How I…", "What I learned…", "On the importance of…"

## Deks (standfirsts)

≤ 30 words. Italic on render. They frame the question without giving away
the punchline.

Good:
- "A theory about distance, another about shame, and an apology that
  arrived eight weeks too late."
- "Forty-one unanswered texts. Not avoiding. Not *not* avoiding. The
  philosophy of *maybe*, applied to friendship."

Avoid:
- Anything that summarises the essay.
- Anything pitched at SEO ("In this essay, I explore…").
- Anything that ends in a question without earning it.

## Length

- Essays (`musings`): 600–1200 words is the sweet spot. Some run shorter.
  None should be longer than 1600.
- Shorts: 400–1000 words. They end on an image, not a moral.

## Pull quotes

Use the `>>>` syntax on a single block:

```
>>> The advertisement asked nothing of me except to be moved.
>>> The friend, somehow, asked *more.*
```

Pull a line that is doing the most work. Never a summary. Never the thesis
stated outright. The line should make sense on its own to someone who
hasn't read the essay yet.

## Handwritten asides

Use `[[aside]]` for a single block of the writer's voice breaking through:

```
[[aside]] I would like a third, kinder theory, please.
```

These are personal, slightly funny, never apologetic. They sound like a
margin note in a journal, not a footnote in a paper.

## Don't do

- "We" — Amit writes in "I".
- "In this essay" / "In this piece" — meta-references.
- "Lessons learned" / "takeaways" / "thoughts" as a header.
- Numbered lists inside essays unless the form genuinely demands it.
- Emoji, emojis as bullet points, em-dash–emoji combos.
- "Tweetable" sentences pulled out as decoration. The whole essay is the unit.
- Quoting yourself. If a line needs to be quoted, you didn't believe it.
- Bullet points in the body. Run-on sentences and short sentences mixed
  intentionally is the form.

## When in doubt

Cut. Tighten. Pick the smaller word. Pick the more specific word. Replace
the abstraction with the object. Replace the explanation with the action.
Replace the universal with the specific Tuesday.

Then italicise the one word that, if removed, would change the meaning.

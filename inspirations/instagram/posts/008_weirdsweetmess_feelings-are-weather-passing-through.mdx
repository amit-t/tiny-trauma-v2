# feelings are weather passing through

## Friction
A four-year-old hands you the emotional regulation you've spent years and a therapist trying to buy, and he just knows it, no source cited.

## Seed line
Feelings are weather passing through. You don't fight weather. You just let it go somewhere. Said by someone who learned it before he learned to lie.

## Voice
observed

## Essay angle
On being out-grown by your own child in the one subject you thought adulthood was supposed to teach you.

## Short angle
A parent writes down what their toddler said about feelings, and the note becomes the thing they read on bad mornings.

## Notable quote
> Feelings were just weather passing through. You don't fight weather. You just let it go somewhere.

## Raw caption
He broke me with one sentence.

Many of us have been in therapy trying to understand exactly this.
He said it casually, like it was the most obvious thing.

Like feelings were just weather passing through.
You don't fight weather. You just let it go somewhere.
I sat there trying to figure out where he heard it.

No book. No person. No cartoon character I could identify.
He said he just knew.
Four years old and already more emotionally literate than most adults I know.
Including me. Especially me.

I'm taking notes from my toddler now.
I did not teach him that but I needed to learn it.
